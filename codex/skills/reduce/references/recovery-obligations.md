# Distinguish dormant capability from redundant burden

Use during an existing Reduce pass when a proposed deletion removes a buffer,
fallback, recovery path, or feedback channel. Reuse the current role, obligation,
candidate, and proof. This reference replaces the retired cybernetic handoff;
it adds no public skill, record, required diagram, or deletion authority. Inside
Actuating, Reduce still nominates under its existing composition.

## Establish the role under the required disturbance

Normal-path inactivity is not evidence that a surface has no work. Identify the
accepted disturbance or recovery obligation, the state/resource it preserves,
and the concrete path that uses it. Bind the claim to the relevant failure model,
reserve, replenishment/consumption rates, and recovery horizon. Do not invent a
disaster requirement to retain an otherwise unsupported abstraction.

Distinguish a reserve that absorbs a burst from a mechanism that restores capacity.
A larger queue may postpone exhaustion without increasing sustainable throughput;
removing all spare capacity may preserve average throughput while losing required
recovery. A finite reserve can be sufficient for a finite accepted job. Size and
timing need workload evidence, not a universal buffer ratio.

For feedback, identify the observation, its age, the decision it changes, and the
actor's actual ability to respond. A second alert that cannot change an action
may be removable; a quiet channel that enables required recovery may not be.
Source and behavior evidence decide the role, not the label "control point."

## Cut the surface only after its obligation is discharged

Compare the incumbent with the proposed lower primitive under the relevant
disturbance and recovery, including pending work and later permitted operations.
Show that the role is unnecessary under the accepted contract, derived from a
retained owner, or preserved by an adequate successor. Name the actual successor
and residual costs. Fewer layers or successful steady-state samples do not prove
the recovery obligation has moved.

For example, a retry supervisor may keep work recoverable across worker failure.
If a durable queue already owns delivery and retries across the same failure
boundary, prove that its lifecycle covers the supervisor's remaining duties before
removing the wrapper. If it only holds an in-memory copy, the similar API does not
establish that coverage. A permanent supervisor may be the rightful owner.

Independent verification and production recovery have different roles. A test can
show that recovery works; it does not perform recovery when production fails.
Conversely, retaining a production fallback does not by itself justify every test
or duplicate guard around it. Apply the existing owner and oracle criteria to each.

If current evidence cannot establish the affected role, return the specific gap
and smallest discriminator under the existing `hold` or nomination contract. Do
not retain every dormant component indefinitely or invoke Universalist inside
Actuating independently. Stop when the cut preserves the accepted obligations
or the missing evidence decides that it cannot yet be justified.

Source: Donella H. Meadows, *Thinking in Systems: A Primer*, edited by Diana
Wright, Earthscan, 2009: stocks and flows pp. 18-24; resilience pp. 76-78;
hierarchy and subsystem purposes pp. 82-85; buffers pp. 149-151. The software
examples and obligation-preserving deletion rule are engineering adaptations.
