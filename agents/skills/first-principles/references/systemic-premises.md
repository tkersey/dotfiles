# Systemic premises

Use only within an already selected First Principles pass when interactions,
accumulated state, or delayed consequences determine premise sufficiency. Apply
the canonical passage unchanged. This reference supplies causal distinctions;
it adds no architecture selector, invocation prerequisite, or mutation authority.

## Make the basis sufficient for the question

Start from the required outcome and deciding observation. When time matters,
establish the relevant behavior over time before explaining it: distinguish a
level, trend, oscillation, and regime change. A local deterministic counterexample
may suffice; do not demand historical data or simulation for every derivation.

Retain relationships needed to recompose the parts. Check only implicated premises:

- Distinguish accumulated state from rates; include initial reserves and pending
  effects when they change the conclusion. Use units and an operational meaning.
- Ask whether an assumed external condition is partly produced by the system's
  own decisions. A contractual limit may be fixed while the remaining capacity
  depends on other actors. External does not mean controllable or negotiable.
- Reconstruct what a decision maker can observe, how old that information is,
  what it optimizes, and which actions/resources it actually controls. Another
  instruction to exercise care does not repair a demonstrated information or
  authority gap; demonstrated capability limitations still matter.
- Distinguish the intended outcome from the proxy reinforced by stopping rules
  or allocation. A perfect-compliance/failed-purpose case can refute their assumed
  equivalence. Observed behavior diagnoses a mismatch; it cannot replace the
  user's objective or retire an independently required procedure.
- State assumed driving conditions separately from the response claimed under
  them. Historical fit or a plausible scenario does not prove that the response
  model survives an intervention. Keep conclusions conditional when inputs are
  unknown; identify the observation that would refute the mechanism itself.

The minimal basis may include interactions, not merely facts about isolated parts.
Do not inherit file, service, or organizational boundaries as the causal partition.
Include a relationship only when omitting it can change the derivation; expand
neither authority nor the implementation merely to enlarge the explanation.

## Worked reconstruction

Replace a solution-shaped premise such as "we need more orchestration" with its
supported observation: completion falls at higher concurrency while per-turn
accuracy is unchanged. Inspect shared limits, outstanding work, and delayed
responses before concluding that the model or supervisor is inadequate.

Separate two accounts: insufficient capability despite adequate inputs/resources,
and decisions that create contention or stale feedback. Use a source argument or
the smallest authorized discriminator that can separate them. Supply the resulting
basis to the existing owner; do not run effects merely to fill this example.

An explicitly required supervisor or audit remains an obligation even if another
mechanism looks simpler. A routine parsing defect with a decisive local witness
does not need a systemic reconstruction. Stop when the basis is sufficient for
the requested derivation or the remaining premise and deciding observation are
explicit; uncertainty is not an excuse to invent a universal causal model.

bounded rationality pp. 106-110; behavior before explanation pp. 170-172.
