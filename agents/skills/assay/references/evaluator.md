# Assess the evaluator

Use when the evaluator is the subject or a concrete mismatch questions the
assessment. Keep the accepted purpose outside the candidate's authority.

## Locate the gap

Separate three questions: does the assessment express the accepted purpose;
do its observations retain the distinction that purpose requires; and do its
rules correctly interpret those observations? Repairing one does not settle
the others.

For an observation gap, seek a concrete pair of cases with the same available
observation but different required judgments under the same accepted question.
A completion message can accompany no effect, one effect, or a duplicate effect.
A total score can conceal which preserved obligation failed. A concrete pair
establishes this inadequacy; failing to find one does not prove adequacy.

Inspect authoritative state or relevant execution facts when available. Final
state can establish an outcome while leaving authorization, sequencing, or
resource obligations unresolved. Choose observations from the obligation,
rather than prescribing one trace shape.

## Preserve both sides

Check meaningful invalid cases and required valid alternatives. Include an
adequate incumbent, legitimate recovery, and justified no-change outcome when
relevant. A reference implementation passing establishes one admitted success.
Rejecting every input or demanding one implementation can fail the purpose.

Derive expected outcomes from the accepted contract and independently supported
domain evidence. A majority of graders, another model's confidence, or a repeated
allegation does not establish that reference. Treat a wrong oracle as a possible
cause; preserve conflicting evidence until the governing question is resolved.

## Reassess carefully

Keep the evaluator and observation definitions identified. Re-score retained
evidence only if it contains the required information; a changed execution
policy ordinarily needs a new execution to establish its behavior.

When candidate and evaluator changes could each explain a result, use the
smallest available contrast that distinguishes them. Comparing old and new
subjects under both evaluators can help when applicable; agreement with the
old evaluator is not itself ground truth.

Assay the evaluator against an explicit trusted basis: controlled fixture
semantics, domain state, an independently checked construction, or identified
human judgments. Do not manufacture an infinite evaluator hierarchy. Record
the basis and its limits in the existing assessment.

Return the supported defect, valid counterexample to the allegation, or exact
missing distinction. A correction remains with the accepted owner and scope.
Do not rewrite the task to make the preferred candidate pass.
